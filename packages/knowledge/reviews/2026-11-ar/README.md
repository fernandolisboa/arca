# Session review 2026-11-ar

Argentina has one stored route, `ar-mercosur-temporary`: temporary residence by nationality for natives of the Mercosur states parties and associated states, Ley 25.871 art. 23, l). Its newest version, 3, effective 2025-05-29, was read on 2026-11-20 against the consolidated Ley 25.871 (current through Decreto 681/2026), Decreto 616/2010, DNU 366/2025, Disposición DNM 1637/2022, Disposiciones DNM 4880/2015 and 6206/2017, the Civil and Commercial Code, Decreto 584/2024, the directorate's fee table and service page, and the Registro Nacional de Reincidencia's page, all on www.argentina.gob.ar ([ADR-0032](../../../../docs/adr/0032-session-reviews-activate-through-a-reviewed-pull-request.md), [runbook](../../../../docs/runbooks/session-rule-review.md)).

Version 3 matched the law on who qualifies (natives of 11 states, naturalized nationals of 8 after five years), the family of art. 23, ñ), the fees (50 UMSM at ARS 1,000, still the unit's value: Decreto 584/2024 has no later change), the fee exemption of unmarried children under 18 and the two years. Two new versions follow it.

## Version 4, correcting version 3 from 2025-05-29

- **Criminal records (art. 29 as replaced by DNU 366/2025).** The law itself now defines a conviction as any sentence, final or not, and a record ("antecedente") as a formal charge (auto de procesamiento) or committal for trial or an equivalent step; both count whatever the manner of serving. This supersedes the narrower definition of Decreto 616/2010, art. 29 (a record was a non-final conviction or a final procesamiento), which still adds that a record abroad counts only when the act is a crime under Argentine law. The three-year threshold is the penalty Argentine law sets for the crime, not the sentence given abroad. `serious-crime` and `lesser-crime-conviction` say so; a new `barred-crimes` holds the absolute bar: no admission, at any penalty, for an intentional crime against life, sexual integrity or the public powers and constitutional order.
- **New assessments:** `other-impediments` (art. 29, a) and e) to l)); `entry-and-presence` (apply online from inside Argentina after a lawful entry, with the sworn statement of purpose and health insurance art. 34 asks since 2025; from abroad a consulate takes it, not modeled); `documents` (Disposición DNM 1637/2022, annex, art. 4, a)); `health-cover` (art. 8: until permanent residence, routine care in health services the national State runs needs insurance or prior payment).
- **`no-entry-ban`** names art. 63: an expulsion for an intentional crime bans reentry for good, without dispensation for the barred crimes.
- **The partner** (and the partner's children) now need both to be adults and unmarried (Civil and Commercial Code, art. 510, a) and d)); a new case shows a married principal brings the spouse, not a partner.
- **The duration** names the cancellation after six months or more abroad (art. 62, e)).
- **New costs, possible:** Argentina's criminal record certificate, ARS 1,000 for the 5-day option, for the principal and each family member aged 16 or more, when it is got outside the online system (as with a preferential appointment); on the standard path the online system includes it.

## Version 5, from 2026-07-31

Decreto 681/2026 added art. 29, m) (hate messages, insulting national symbols) from the day after its publication on 2026-07-30. Version 5 is version 4 with m) in `other-impediments`.

## Rounds

Two reviewers in rounds 1 and 2, one checking each item against its provision, the other looking for anything official that makes the route out of date; one reviewer in round 3, on the changes.

- **Round 1**, on a single version 4. Proven and fixed: art. 29, m) cannot apply before 2026-07-31 (now version 5); the absolute bar for barred crimes applies at any penalty (now `barred-crimes`); "record" includes a formal charge, and a suspended sentence counts; the consular path abroad exists; a minor, or a married person, cannot be in a cohabiting union; cancellation after six months abroad; the health-care rule of art. 8; the certificate cost; the sworn statement.
- **Round 2.** Proven and fixed: the partner must also be unmarried; the certificate is included in the online system on the standard path, so its cost is possible only; art. 63's permanent ban; art. 8 covers only national services. The second reviewer found nothing that makes either version out of date: Disposición DNM 1547/2026 (2026-11-06) only repeals the precarious residence for people under expulsion orders, Disposición DNM 34/2026 concerns fines, and the certificate's price is unchanged.
- **Round 3**, on those changes: no defect. One wording note kept: `no-entry-ban` shortens "the public powers and constitutional order" to "the constitutional order" for room; `barred-crimes` spells it out.

The proposal check and the quote check, which reads the official pages through the app's reader (`ARCA_REVIEW_QUOTES=2026-11-ar`), pass on the final files.

## Leftover

1. **The consular path.** An application through an Argentine consulate costs 300 consular units, whose value no text read gives; the versions model applying in Argentina.
2. **Fees not modeled:** the extension or change to permanent residence (50 UMSM, to file within 60 days before expiry, 50% surcharge up to 30 days late, per the directorate's FAQ); the preferential appointment (50 UMSM); the first DNI for foreigners, whose RENAPER tariff Resolución 151/2026 replaces from 2026-11-21 with an annex the session could not read; exemptions for indigence, minors under official protection, 30 years in the country, refugees, and the free certificate for a poverty certificate. Fees in a unit (UMSM) still need a new version whenever the unit's value changes ([OPEN-QUESTIONS](../../../../docs/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md)).
3. **Facts no route records:** a recognized refugee applies under art. 23, k), not l); a naturalization's age (five years); a registered union and when cohabitation began (`edge.start` is the relationship's start); the partner's age for the partner's children (a registered union implies adults); a minor emancipated by a marriage that ended; an adult child's disability; whether a child applies together with a parent (fee exemption).
4. **Not in the titles, for lack of room:** a conviction's record lapses under Penal Code art. 51 (Decreto 616/2010, art. 29); a pending charge suspends the application, with a precarious residence meanwhile (Decreto 616/2010, art. 20, b)); the directorate weighs specially Mercosur natives who entered irregularly (Decreto 616/2010, art. 29); minors' papers (parental authorization before a migration officer, legalized birth certificate), legalization and translation, the electronic address, and no foreign certificate when the last entry was before age 15 (Disposición DNM 1637/2022, annex, arts. 6 and 7); the other grounds of cancellation in art. 62 (an intentional-crime conviction; from 2026-07-31, art. 62, h)); family members' own stay under art. 23, ñ) (three years or the resident's period).
5. **Panama.** Uruguay's presidency reports Panama joined Mercosur as an associated state in December 2024, and Decreto 616/2010 counts every associated state, but the directorate's Disposición 1637/2022 and service page still list 11 states, and no Argentine official page read confirms it; mercosur.int and cancilleria.gob.ar refuse the sandbox.
6. **The duration.** The law grants two years; the directorate's page says "at most two years". The versions keep two years.
7. **Leap day.** `unsettled`, as in version 3: the Civil and Commercial Code's art. 6 counts periods, and no text read says when a person born on 29 February turns a year older.
8. **Production rows were not read.** This session has no database credentials; the proof of publishing is the production build, which runs the migration.
