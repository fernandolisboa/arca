# Session review 2026-11-us-employer

A session review that adds two routes ([ADR-0032, amendment 2026-11-25](../../../../docs/adr/0032-session-reviews-activate-through-a-reviewed-pull-request.md#amendment-2026-11-25-new-routes), [runbook](../../../../docs/runbooks/session-rule-review.md)) for the employer-sponsored green card, on the `us` official-domain list the stored U.S. routes already pin. Both are version 1, from 2026-01-01, the day Proclamation 10998 took effect; the fees they state date from the USCIS fee rule of 2024-04-01 (89 FR 6194). Every source was read on 2026-11-25, São Paulo date: 8 U.S.C. 1101, 1153, 1182 and 1202 on govinfo (the 2024 edition), Proclamation 10998 on govinfo (90 FR 59717), and on the eCFR, current to 2026-11-20, 8 CFR 103.2, 106.1, 106.2, 204.5, 213a.2, 245.2, 245.5 and 245.25, 20 CFR 656.3, 656.5, 656.10, 656.12, 656.15, 656.17, 656.30 and 656.40, and 22 CFR 22.1 and 42.63 to 42.66. The Federal Register was searched for rules since 2024 on labor certification, prevailing wages, Form I-140 and these fees; the eCFR's history shows 20 CFR part 656 unchanged since 2021 and 8 CFR 204.5 since 2024-04-01.

## The routes

- **`us-eb3`, EB-3.** Permanent residence for a skilled worker (a job needing at least 2 years of training or experience), a professional (a job needing a bachelor's, which the worker holds) or another worker (less; at most 10,000 visas a year), on a U.S. employer's permanent, full-time job offer and an individual labor certification from the Department of Labor, or a Schedule A application with DHS (8 U.S.C. 1153(b)(3); 8 CFR 204.5(l); 20 CFR part 656).
- **`us-eb2-job-offer`, EB-2 with a job offer.** The same process for a member of the professions with an advanced degree (or a bachelor's and five years of progressive experience after it) or someone of exceptional ability, when the job itself requires one or the other (8 U.S.C. 1153(b)(2)(A); 8 CFR 204.5(k)(4)(i)). It is its own route because the Act gives EB-2 and EB-3 separate visa numbers and queues; `us-eb2-niw` is the variant without a job offer.

**What decides.** The one requirement reads the case's job offers ([#510](https://github.com/fernandolisboa/arca/issues/510)): some offer is in the U.S., not temporary, and the employer will sponsor it. An offer elsewhere, a temporary one, an unsponsored one or none reads not eligible; an unanswered sponsorship or no job offers recorded reads missing information. Everything else is a judgment the case cannot record and stays an assessment: the labor certification itself, Schedule A, a bona fide full-time job (with the employer's burden when the worker owns part of it, is related to its owners or is one of few employees), the prevailing wage, the employer's ability to pay, the worker meeting the job's requirements and the category (EB-3), the advanced degree or exceptional ability and the job requiring it (EB-2), a foreign medical graduate's exams, a health care worker's certificate, Proclamation 10998, and visa availability by priority date.

**Family.** The spouse and unmarried children under 21, with adopted children and stepchildren as 8 U.S.C. 1101(b)(1) admits them, copied from `us-eb2-niw` v1 (8 U.S.C. 1153(d)).

**Costs.** The Form I-140 fee and the Asylum Program Fee the employer pays with it (USD 300 for a small employer, none for a nonprofit; the version counts USD 600); the immigrant visa and USCIS immigrant fees per person when the family gets its visas abroad; the Form I-485 per person, possible, when it lives in the U.S.; and the USD 120 affidavit of support review fee, possible. The labor certification has no government fee, and 20 CFR 656.12(b) bars the employer from taking payment for obtaining it, which the labor certification document's note says.

**Documents** ([#519](https://github.com/fernandolisboa/arca/issues/519)). The petition's: Form I-140, the approved labor certification (original, signed by the employer, filed within 180 days), the employer's ability-to-pay evidence, experience letters, and the degree record (EB-3 professional; EB-2 advanced degree) or the exceptional ability evidence (EB-2); the health care worker's certificate and the relative's affidavit of support where they apply; for adjustment in the U.S., Form I-485, the civil surgeon's Form I-693 and Supplement J; and for a visa abroad the consular list `us-eb5` v10 holds (22 CFR 42.63 to 42.66, 8 U.S.C. 1202 and 1182(a)(1)(A)(ii)). No Brazil document guide changes: the new kinds (`labor_certification`, `employer_financial_evidence`, `employment_reference_letter`, `exceptional_ability_evidence`, `health_care_worker_certificate`, `affidavit_of_support`, `adjustment_of_status_form`, `job_offer_confirmation`) have no Brazilian issuer.

## Rounds

Two adversarial reviewers read every item against the downloaded official text and searched the eCFR's history and the Federal Register. Round 1 found no wrong amount and no wrong requirement, and these, all fixed: Proclamation 10998, which suspends immigrant entry for nationals of listed countries, was not modeled (now an assessment, and the versions start on its effective date); the adjustment path had fees but no documents (Form I-485, Form I-693, Supplement J added); the affidavit of support review fee was missing; the priority date of a Schedule A petition is its USCIS filing date, not a Department of Labor date; the 10,000 visas for other workers are a ceiling; Schedule A's conditions; the employer's burden and the three triggers of a bona fide job (20 CFR 656.17(l)); the requirement's title for Schedule A filings; four looser titles; and the pt-BR words for an establishment and an advanced degree. Round 2 found the proclamation's title still left out whom section 6 spares, a skilled worker's two years unquoted, the portability conditions of Supplement J, the performing arts under Schedule A and the 8 CFR 245.2 citation for Form I-485, all fixed. Round 3 read those fixes and found two low points, listed below. The app's quote check (`ARCA_REVIEW_QUOTES=2026-11-us-employer`, in `apps/api`) passed in the session after each round; the `review-quotes` job runs it again on the pull request.

## Leftover

1. **Facts the case cannot hold as evidence.** An assessment's evidence may name only principal or unit facts, not a job offer's salary or occupation, so the prevailing wage, ability to pay and the job's requirements cite the nearest facts (income, education) as context, never as proof. The offer's salary is not compared with the prevailing wage, which the Department of Labor sets per job and area on a site outside the `us` list.
2. **Proclamation 10998 as an assessment.** A session review cannot add a country set, so the suspension is a judgment on the principal's citizenships, not a requirement over each person; dependents' nationalities count only through the title. A Brazilian national is not on its lists. Court orders against it could not be checked on official sources.
3. **Visa availability.** EB-2 and EB-3 numbers for the rest of the world have backlogs in the Visa Bulletin, on `travel.state.gov`, which refuses this session. No duration models the wait, nor the Department of Labor's processing times.
4. **Children's age.** As in `us-eb2-niw`, the age is tested today, while 8 U.S.C. 1153(h) counts it when a visa number becomes available, less the petition's pending time.
5. **Fees the case cannot pick.** The Asylum Program Fee counts USD 600, though a small employer pays USD 300 and a nonprofit nothing; the case does not record the employer's size. For a family living in the U.S., the consular fees and the Form I-485 are both possible, so the possible extra counts both although only one applies. The USD 50 online filing discount (8 CFR 106.1(g)), optional premium processing and, for a family already in the U.S., any work or travel permits are not costs here.
6. **Not in force, not modeled.** Department of Labor proposals 91 FR 73916 (2026-11-17: a new labor market test, and the end of Schedule A) and 91 FR 15454 (2026-03-27: higher prevailing wage levels); the proposed end of the 60-day grace period (91 FR 57807). The signature rule in force since 2026-07-10 (8 CFR 103.2(a)(2)), the public charge rule (91 FR 45324) and the State Department's 2026 pauses on immigrant visas are not modeled, as in `us-eb5`.
7. **After round 3.** The EB-2 route's `exceptional-ability` assessment keeps `us-eb2-niw`'s title, "3 of 6 kinds of evidence", though its quotes state only "at least three of the following" (8 CFR 204.5(k)(3)(ii) lists six). The pt-BR texts for an advanced degree now say "título acima do bacharelado (como mestrado ou doutorado)", since the rule's advanced degree includes professional degrees; no reviewer read that last wording.
8. **Production rows were not read.** This session has no database credentials; the proof of publishing is the production build, which runs the migration and fails if the database already holds one of these routes.
