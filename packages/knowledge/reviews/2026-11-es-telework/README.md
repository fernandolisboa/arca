# Session review 2026-11-es-telework

A session review that adds a route ([ADR-0032, amendment 2026-11-25](../../../../docs/adr/0032-session-reviews-activate-through-a-reviewed-pull-request.md#amendment-2026-11-25-new-routes), [runbook](../../../../docs/runbooks/session-rule-review.md)). Spain held one route, `es-non-lucrative`. This review writes `es-international-telework` version 1, from 2024-09-24 (the date Orden AUC/891/2024's EUR 90 type D visa fee applies to every application), on the `es` official-domain list `es-non-lucrative` already pins, with the `eu-free-movement` country set at number 2. Every source was read on boe.es on 2026-11-25, São Paulo date: Ley 14/2013 (consolidated; arts. 61, 62, 74 bis to 74 quinquies, 75, 76, nineteenth and twentieth additional provisions, thirteenth final provision, none amended since Ley 11/2023 took effect on 2023-05-10), Ley Orgánica 4/2000, Real Decreto 240/2007, Orden AUC/891/2024, Orden ESS/1571/2014 and the Código Civil. The BOE was searched for later changes and orders on these provisions.

## The route

International telework (digital nomad): a third-country national who works remotely, only by telematic means, for companies seated outside Spain (Ley 14/2013, art. 74 bis, added by Ley 28/2022). Living abroad, the applicant asks a consulate for the telework visa, valid up to a year (art. 74 quater); lawfully in Spain, or in the 60 days before the visa expires, the residence permit, up to three years and renewable for two at a time (art. 74 quinquies).

- **Requirements the case decides.** Not a citizen of Spain, another EU or EEA state or Switzerland (art. 61.2); 18 or older (art. 62.3 b); and, from the work periods (#509), a relationship with companies outside Spain for the last three months that still goes on: an employee with the same company since at least three months ago (art. 74 ter c), or a contractor or self-employed person whose clients outside Spain cover the last three months unbroken (art. 74 ter d). A business owner working for their own company abroad reads as possible.
- **Assessments.** The company's year of real activity, the documents showing the work can be remote, the qualification (a degree from a university or business school of recognized prestige, vocational training, or three years of experience), the limit on work for Spanish companies, sufficient means, health insurance, criminal record, irregular stay, refusable listing, public order, and which path (visa or permit) applies.
- **Family (art. 62.4).** Spouse; partner in a relationship akin to marriage; children under 18; adult children who depend on the holder and have not formed their own family unit (possible, since having children of their own is not a fact the case records); dependent parents and grandparents. The spouse's and partner's children on the same terms, and the spouse's and partner's dependent parents, are possible: the law does not say whose.
- **Costs.** The EUR 90 type D visa fee for each applicant applying from abroad (Orden AUC/891/2024, applied through Ley 14/2013's thirteenth final provision and LO 4/2000).
- **Documents.** The ones Ley 14/2013 asks applicants to prove: the company's year of activity, that the work can be remote, the employment or commercial relationship of the last three months, the qualification, criminal record proof and the sworn statement, health insurance (also for each family member, art. 62.4) and proof of means for the family. Kinds reuse those other routes already use (`police_certificate`, `health_insurance` and `means_of_subsistence_proof` as in `es-non-lucrative` v5; `employment_contract` and `service_contract` as in `pt-d8`); new kinds: `company_activity_proof`, `remote_work_proof`, `qualification_proof`, `criminal_record_statement`. No document guide entry is added: none of these is a Brazilian civil record the guide covers.

## Rounds

Two adversarial reviewers, one reading every item against the downloaded provisions, one searching the BOE for anything that makes the route out of date or incomplete.

- **Round 1** found three proven errors, all fixed: the spouse's and partner's children and the spouse's parents were possible with no condition (now on the law's terms of age and dependence, plus the open question of whose); the fee source cited art. 49.1 instead of 46.1 of LO 4/2000; and the visa fee order's date of application sits in its final provision, not article 1. It also found that Orden ESS/1571/2014 sets the fee of Ley 14/2013's residence permits (EUR 70.40 initial, 75.60 renewed, raised each year by budget laws), which the first draft said was not found; that the documents missed the criminal record, insurance and means proofs and the family members' items; and loose wording (prestige applies to universities and business schools only; "over 18"; where the means instructions are published). All fixed. The professional and owner branches now also need a period that still goes on, like the employee's.
- **Round 2** found that the regenerated cases had overwritten the gap fixture, one document title kept the old wording, and the permit fee figures in the assessment title had no quote. All fixed; the visa fee titles now name the order behind the amount.
- **Round 3** read the delta and the Portuguese texts and found no errors; two wording points in the Portuguese were taken.

The app's quote check (`ARCA_REVIEW_QUOTES=2026-11-es-telework`, in `apps/api`) passed in the session after each round. The `review-quotes` job runs it again on the pull request.

## Leftover

1. **The means threshold.** Ley 14/2013's twentieth additional provision refers it to the minimum wage (SMI) and leaves the amount to instructions of the competent bodies, which are not in the BOE; the figures are on inclusion.gob.es and exteriores.gob.es, not on Spain's official-domain list. Sufficient means stays an assessment.
2. **The permit fee in Spain.** Orden ESS/1571/2014 states EUR 70.40 (initial) and 75.60 (renewed) as 2014 amounts, raised by the yearly budget laws; no boe.es page states today's amount, so it is in the assessment's title and not a cost. The foreigner identity card (TIE, Ley 14/2013 art. 76.2, for a permit over six months) and its fee are left out: Orden PJC/617/2025's card fees reach it only through the thirteenth final provision, by interpretation.
3. **Deadlines in business days.** The visa decision (10 business days, art. 75.5), the permit decision (20 days with positive silence, art. 76.1) and the 90-day late renewal (art. 76.3) are not durations, which hold calendar periods only.
4. **Readings kept.** An employee with the same company abroad for the three months meets the requirement (art. 74 ter c says "la empresa"); one whose three months span two employers abroad back to back reads as possible, since a move inside a group of companies (art. 74 ter a) cannot be told from a change of employer. A contractor's clients must cover the three months with no gap. An adult child with children of their own, or a dependent child's own family unit, cannot be told from the case, so such children are possible, never certain.
5. **What the consulates ask on top.** Passport, application form, photographs, translations and apostilles are in the consulates' lists on exteriores.gob.es, not in the law and not on the `es` list, so the documents list holds only what Ley 14/2013 asks.
6. **Reciprocity.** The visa fee for Brazilians may differ by reciprocity (LO 4/2000 art. 48.4), which is not checked.
7. **No version before 2024-09-24.** The route exists since Ley 28/2022 took effect on 2022-12-23, but version 1 starts when the visa fee it states applies, so earlier dates have no version; the review researched only the rules in force today.
8. **Corrections after the third round, which no adversarial reviewer saw.** Two wording points in the Portuguese texts (the sworn statement's name, a gloss on vocational training dropped), "conta como possível" in the requirement's Portuguese, and, after the repository's reviewers, the employee and professional documents now follow the work periods (shown when an ongoing period with a company abroad has that relation, possible for an owner) instead of always possible. After the correctness reviewer: back-to-back employers abroad over the three months read as possible (leftover 4), the partner's dependent parents are possible like the spouse's, and each family member's visa fee follows that member's own country of residence, not the principal's; new cases cover these, a self-employed applicant with a client in Spain, and a contractor whose only client is in Spain.
9. **Production rows were not read.** This session has no database credentials; the proof of publishing is the production build, which runs the migration and fails if the database already holds this route.
