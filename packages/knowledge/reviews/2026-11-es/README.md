# Session review 2026-11-es

Spain has one stored route, `es-non-lucrative` (the non-lucrative residence visa, for people who live on their own means without working). Version 3, effective 2025-06-16, was read against the consolidated texts on `boe.es` on 2026-11-20. Version 4 corrects it, from the same date ([ADR-0032](../../../../docs/adr/0032-session-reviews-activate-through-a-reviewed-pull-request.md), [runbook](../../../../docs/runbooks/session-rule-review.md)).

## What version 4 changes

- **Means, whose they are.** Version 3 added up the passive income of everyone in the family and the household's savings. Real Decreto 1155/2024, article 62.1, asks the applicant to have the means "para sí mismo y, en su caso, su familia", and has no clause that adds up what each relative has, as article 76.2 has for another permit. The principal's own passive income at the monthly amount, or the principal's own savings at the yearly amount, now settles the requirement. A family that reaches the amount only with a relative's income or savings reads as "Faltam informações", and the title says why.
- **Means, the mix.** Version 3 took twelve months of income plus savings against twelve monthly amounts. Article 62.1 names two ways, means for the permit's period "o" a periodic income, and no text states the twelve in figures, which the quote check needs for a factor (leftover 1). The yearly amount is now written with the yearly IPREM the law states, EUR 7,200 (Ley 31/2022, additional provision 90, c)). A family with some income and some savings, neither enough alone, reads as "Faltam informações" unless a month of income plus all the savings is under one monthly amount, when no mix can reach twelve.
- **Means, other proofs.** The title now says that the proofs article 62.3 also admits (property titles, certified cheques, credit on a card) are not counted, and a source for that paragraph is added (leftover 2).
- **Partner.** Version 3 took a partnership that began a year ago as proof. Article 61.3 a) asks for a registered partner, or a stable one "debidamente probada", and presumes the bond after a year of continuous cohabitation, or with children in common and proven stable cohabitation. The case records only when the partnership began, so a partner, and with them the partner's children, now always read as possible.
- **Married minors.** Version 3 left out a child under 18 who is married. Article 61.3 b) says "no casados, o que no hayan constituido su propia unidad familiar", which may take in a married minor who has not formed a family unit of their own. Such a child now reads as possible.
- **New items.** The bars of Ley Orgánica 4/2000, fourth additional provision, 1 c), d) and e) (a repeated application, an expulsion order or proceeding, an entry ban); where the visa is applied for and collected (article 26.1); and one month to collect the visa, after which it is taken as given up (article 28.4).
- **Criminal record.** The assessment also cites Ley Orgánica 4/2000, article 31.5, which asks for no record in Spain or in earlier countries of residence with no limit of five years.
- **Titles.** Assessments say they bind each applicant, not only the principal (the assessment still shows the principal's fact). The adult children titles name the health condition of article 61.3 c). The citizenship title no longer says that Spanish citizens live in Spain under EU rules: they are not foreigners (Ley Orgánica 4/2000, article 1.1). The permit decision counts from the receipt of the consulate's communication (article 63.4).
- **Sources.** Every title checked against its provision, every `verifiedAt` is 2026-11-20, and the route pins version 2 of the country set `eu-free-movement`, whose pages are the ones that still answer.

The amounts (IPREM of EUR 600 a month, fees of EUR 90, 10.94 and 16.08), the other durations, the spouse rule and the other assessments of version 3 matched their sources. Nothing published up to the BOE of 2026-11-20 amends a provision the route cites: Real Decreto 316/2026 and the Supreme Court judgments of July 2026 touch other articles of the regulation, Real Decreto-ley 38/2026 sets no IPREM and no fee, and no budget law for 2024 to 2027 exists.

## Rounds

Three adversarial rounds, the limit, by two reviewers: one read each item against its provision, the other looked for anything official that makes the route out of date or incomplete (the later references of each cited act, the BOE's search by title, the daily summaries of 2026-11-10 to 2026-11-20). Round 1: nothing out of date; the partner and the married minor decided what the text leaves open; the means added up the relatives' means and failed families on other proofs; three items were missing; five titles were inexact. Round 2, on the corrected files: no wrong item among the new ones; the titles of the means, the partner and the expulsion bar were reworded, and the assessments now name each applicant; what it found and the schema cannot hold is in the leftover list (1, 2, 4, 5 and 6). Round 3 read the titles changed in round 2 and found three inexact: the title on where to apply let a service provider or an online filing collect the visa, and the two wordings of the expulsion bar dropped the unchanged circumstances and half of the exception. They were reworded after it (leftover 16). The quote check, the app's own (`ARCA_REVIEW_QUOTES=2026-11-es`, in `apps/api`), passed in the session on 2026-11-20 UTC: every item matches on the pages it cites and its quotes state its values. The `review-quotes` job runs it again on the pull request.

## Leftover

1. **The mix of income and savings has only a weak floor.** Articles 62.2 and 61.4 give the yearly amount as the monthly one over a permit of one year, so twelve months of income plus savings under the yearly amount cannot qualify. The factor twelve is in no cited text in figures or words, and the quote check takes a factor only from a quote, so the expression does not multiply by it. A couple with one child, EUR 100 a month and EUR 3,500 saved reads "Faltam informações" where "not eligible" is certain. So does a family with no income whose savings are known only as a range that crosses the monthly amount, since the test for some income compares two sums that hold the same savings. No decided result is wrong.
2. **Other proofs are not counted.** Article 62.3 admits property titles, certified cheques and the credit available on a card. No fact records them, and a requirement must be able to fail, so a family short on recorded income and savings reads "not eligible" even if it owns property. The title says so.
3. **Means from shares in companies in Spain** need a certificate that the person does no work in them (article 62.3, last paragraph). It is a document, and no fact records where passive income comes from.
4. **The amount counts every dependent the family rules include.** A principal whose means cover fewer relatives than the family has reads "not eligible", though the visa is open to a smaller group (article 62.1 b) asks 100% per relative "a su cargo, durante su residencia en España").
5. **EU, EEA and Swiss citizens read "not eligible".** Ley Orgánica 4/2000, article 1.3, still applies the law to them "en aquellos aspectos que pudieran ser más favorables". The accepted reading since version 2 is that their own regime is always the easier path. Spanish citizens are not foreigners at all (article 1.1).
6. **Family members of an EU citizen** are under the EU regime whatever their own citizenship (Real Decreto 240/2007, article 2). The requirement reads only the principal's citizenships, so a Brazilian principal married to an Italian reads as eligible ([OPEN-QUESTIONS.md](../../../../docs/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md)).
7. **An unmarried minor with a family unit of their own** reads as a sure dependent. Read as two conditions, article 61.3 b) would leave them out; the case does not record such a unit.
8. **Assessments show the principal's fact only.** Articles 38 and 61.2 bind each person who applies, and the titles say so, but the facts shown as evidence are the principal's.
9. **The IPREM's proof ends with 2026.** Additional provision 90 sets the amounts "durante 2023", and Orden EFD/581/2026 states the extension for 2024, 2025 and 2026. With no budget law, the 2023 budget stays extended in 2027, as Real Decreto-ley 38/2026, article 1.1, assumes; the route needs a new version when a text states the 2027 extension or a new IPREM.
10. **The identity card fee follows the annex.** Orden PJC/617/2025, article 1.3, says in general terms that identity card fees equal the fee of the national identity document, and its annex gives EUR 12 only for the cards of relatives of Spanish citizens (4.1) and EUR 16.08 for the card of a first temporary residence permit (4.2). The cost is the annex's EUR 16.08.
11. **The visa fee by reciprocity** (Ley Orgánica 4/2000, article 48.4) is on no listed host; the EUR 90 cost says another amount may apply.
12. **Deadlines of the procedure itself** are not durations: ten working days to pay the permit and card fees (Orden PJC/617/2025, article 2.5), and the consulate's summons and requests (Real Decreto 1155/2024, article 27.3 and 27.5).
13. **Renewal** (article 64) is not modeled beyond the length of the renewed permit.
14. **Versions 1 to 3** were not reviewed: version 4 replaces version 3 from 2025-06-16, and version 2 still holds 2025-05-20 to 2025-06-15, a past period that an evaluation made today does not select.
15. **Production rows were not read.** This session has no database credentials; the proof of publishing is the production build, which runs the migration and fails if the database already holds a version 4.
16. **Three wordings no reviewer saw.** Round 3 was the last round, so the titles written after it were checked only by the session, the proposal check and the quote check: the assessment `application-at-own-consulate` (the visa is collected in person; article 26.1 also lets an accredited representative collect it where the rules provide for one, which the title leaves out), the assessment `no-entry-ban-or-expulsion` and the source `lo-4-2000-inadmission` (an expulsion order bars admission unless it was revoked or falls under articles 31 bis, 59, 59 bis or 68.3 of the law, which both titles shorten to "exceptions"). After the repository's reviewers read the pull request, five titles were reworded for plain English with no change of meaning (the means requirement, the citizenship requirement, the partner's children, and the same assessment and source of the expulsion bar); the checks ran again on them.
