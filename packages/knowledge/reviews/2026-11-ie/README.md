## Version 4, correcting version 3 from 2026-06-12

- **Family, from the Policy's paras. 1.5, 6.3, 7.21 to 7.26, 8.3 and 10.15 to 10.21:**
  - `parents` (new): a parent who depends on the principal is possible. A Category B sponsor applies for family other than the nuclear family only after 2 years in the State; version 3 left parents out entirely.
  - `children`: an adult child dependent on the principal for a serious medical or psychological condition (para. 7.23) is now possible; a child under 18 with a partner is possible instead of included, since a partnership the case records may be a civil partnership, which counts as marriage (para. 7.10), and only unmarried children count (para. 7.22).
  - `spouse-children`: the same terms as the principal's children, since the Policy's "child" includes a step-child.
  - `partner-children` (new): a de facto partner's children on the same terms, but only possible: the Policy names a "step-child of the sponsor" without saying whether a partner's child is one.
  - `spouse`: the title adds the 5-year wait before a new spouse after a divorce (para. 7.11), not checked.
- **New assessments:** `dependent-adult-relatives` (the relative first gets Stamp 0, a permission to stay that does not allow work, Irish gross income above €96,929 in each of the 3 previous years for one adult and €130,985 for two in 2026, private medical insurance; Appendix D) and `family-housing` (para. 9.4: no homeless, emergency, IPAS, social or local authority housing).
- **Titles:** `pay` says only basic salary and health insurance count, not bonuses (the Department's page); `employer-staff` adds a bona fide employer trading in Ireland and registered with the Revenue Commissioners; `application-fee` says the worker, the employer or a third party may pay, and an employer who applies may not recover it from the worker (Employment Permits Act 2024, s. 55, as the Department's page cites it); `stamp-4` says the Stamp 4 may be applied for after 21 months of employment on the permit (Immigration Service Delivery's Stamp 4 page, now cited), not at the end of it.
- **Sources** all read again on 2026-11-20 and dated so; every item cites its passages in `ie-critical-skills.4.evidence.json`.

## Rounds

Two reviewers in round 1, one checking each item against its provision, the other looking for anything official that makes the route out of date; the first again in rounds 2 and 3, on the changes. Then the nine repository reviewers.

- **Round 1.** No amount, age, date or citation wrong. Proven and fixed: a dependent adult stepchild was excluded though the Policy's "child" includes a step-child (para. 7.23 with the glossary); the Appendix D figures had no quote; a child under 18 with a partner was a certain family member though the partnership may be a civil partnership; the employer may apply and pay, so the fee title no longer charges the worker alone; the Stamp 4 opens after 21 months of employment (Immigration Service Delivery's Stamp 4 page), not at the end of the permit; the bona fide employer registered with the Revenue Commissioners was missing. The second reviewer found no newer instrument, act or policy that changes an amount; the International Protection Act 2026 lowers the registration age to 6 but leaves the fee waiver under 18.
- **Round 2**, on the changes: the fee title said whoever applies pays, while a third party may pay, and dropped the refund on withdrawal; the private medical insurance and the word "gross" had lost their quotes; no case covered a spouse's adult child whose dependence is unknown. All fixed.
- **Round 3**, on the round-2 changes: the employer's ban on recovering the fee holds only where the employer applied; fixed.
- **Repository reviewers** (correctness, security, architecture, spec, standards, context, data access, auth and cryptography, AI safety): all passed, with low points. Fixed after them, so no adversarial reviewer saw these: the adult child's 2 years in the `children` title, the fee title's "who applies", "is not a non-national" in `not-british-citizen`, and wording in this record and the route's note.

## Leftover

1. **The partnership's start.** `partner` counts the 2 years from the start of the union the case records; the Policy asks for 2 years of cohabitation, which the case does not record separately. Immigration Service Delivery also keeps a page for the de facto partner of a Critical Skills holder, under a 2019 policy updated in June 2023, that asks for 2 years living together, medical insurance, police certificates and no other partner sponsored in 7 years; whether the June 2026 Policy replaced it is not stated, so a shorter relationship stays possible and the 7 years are not modeled.
2. **Stepchildren in the household.** The spouse's children count as family members though living full time in the household (para. 7.26) is not checked; the partner's children are only possible.
3. **The family's own permission and work.** The Policy's Appendix C gives the spouse, partner and children Stamp 1G and dependent adult relatives Stamp 0; the version models who may come, not what they may do.
4. **Permit conditions after the grant**, such as the 9 months with the first employer (S.I. No. 444 of 2024, regs. 18 and 19), are not eligibility and are not modeled.
5. **Schedule 3's dates.** The occupation assessment cites S.I. No. 558 of 2026, made on 28 October 2026, though the version starts on 12 June 2026, when S.I. No. 213 of 2026's list applied; it is an assessment, so no result changes.
6. **2027 thresholds.** The minimum annual remuneration roadmap leaves January 2027 to be determined; a version 5 is due once new amounts are made law.
7. **The spouse's parents.** Only the principal's own parents are modeled; whether the Policy admits a spouse's dependent parent was not settled.
8. **Production rows were not read.** This session has no database credentials; the proof of publishing is the production build, which runs the migration.
