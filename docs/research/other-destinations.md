## Spain

The non-lucrative residence visa is now route version `es-non-lucrative` 1 in `packages/knowledge/data/routes`, verified against the BOE on 2026-11-06: Real Decreto 1155/2024, articles 61 and 62, with the IPREM of EUR 600 a month from Ley 31/2022, whose 2023 budget stays extended. A registered partner, a partner with children in common and adult children who need support show as possible, since the case does not record those facts. Version 2, added on 2026-11-07, corrects version 1 from its first day: Ley Orgánica 4/2000 (article 1) and Real Decreto 240/2007 (article 1 and third additional provision) put Spanish, other EU and EEA, and Swiss citizens under the EU regime, so a principal who holds any of those citizenships is not eligible. Accepted reading: article 1.3 still applies the law to them where it is more favourable, but their own regime (registration under Real Decreto 240/2007) is always the easier path, so the visa is shown as not for them. The digital nomad visa (Ley 14/2013, article 74 bis) waits: its income threshold, a percentage of the minimum wage, is set in instructions on inclusion.gob.es, which refuses automated reads.

## Uruguay

Mercosur permanent residence is now route version `uy-mercosur-permanent` 1 in `packages/knowledge/data/routes`, verified on 2026-11-07 against IMPO and the migration directorate's pages on gub.uy. Each listed national qualifies on their own nationality, so a Brazilian child qualifies as a Brazilian. A family member from outside the listed states comes in through family reunification: a spouse, a partner of five years or more, an unmarried child or stepchild, or a parent who depends on a principal from a state party to the 2002 Residence Agreements (Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Paraguay) is a dependent; anyone else in those relationships shows as possible. Accepted readings: art. 9.2 of the Agreements does not define family members and sets no age, so the route reads it as covering a dependent unmarried adult child and a dependent unmarried stepchild, while art. 10 of the migration law lists only children who are minors or adults with a disability; a later version should narrow this if the directorate does. The five years of a partnership count from the start of the union the case records, since it records no date for living together, which Ley 18.246 counts.

## Argentina

Temporary residence by nationality (Mercosur) is now route version `ar-mercosur-temporary` 1 in `packages/knowledge/data/routes`, verified on 2026-11-07 against the consolidated texts and the migration directorate's page on www.argentina.gob.ar. It grants two years, renewable. Each listed national qualifies on their own nationality if it is native, so a Brazilian child born a Brazilian qualifies as a Brazilian. A family member from anywhere comes in through family reunification with the temporary resident. Accepted readings: a citizenship counts as native when it is by birth, or by descent or another basis acquired on the birth date (the new fact `person.citizenshipsFromBirth`); a Brazilian citizenship by descent recorded with a later date shows as possible. A partner counts after two years from the start of the union the case records, since the case records neither the date living together began nor the registration; the registration only proves the union (Civil and Commercial Code, art. 511), so it is read as a document, like a marriage certificate, which no route checks yet. The spouse's children are read as joining the spouse, who is a temporary resident by reunification; no other chained relative is modeled.

## Paraguay

Mercosur temporary residence is now route version `py-mercosur-temporary` 1 in `packages/knowledge/data/routes`, verified on 2026-11-07 against the laws on www.bacn.gov.py and the migration directorate's page on migraciones.gov.py. It grants two years, not renewable; the holder may then ask for Mercosur permanent residence (Ley 3578, art. 5), which is not a route yet. Each listed national qualifies on their own nationality if it is native, so a Brazilian child born a Brazilian qualifies as a Brazilian. A family member without a listed nationality comes in through art. 9.2 of the Residence Agreement: a spouse, an unmarried child or stepchild under 18, or a parent who depends on a principal native of a state that signed the agreement Paraguay approved by Ley 3578 (Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Uruguay) is a dependent; anyone else in those relationships shows as possible. Accepted readings: the agreement does not define family members, so the route takes the list in the migration law's reunification principle (Ley 6984, art. 4.10: parents, spouses, unmarried minor children, adult children with a disability) and reads a dependent stepchild as a child, as the Uruguay and Argentina versions do. That list names no partner, and Ley 6984 never mentions a stable union, so a partner is not a dependent, not even a possible one: a rule that can only answer "not known" cannot pass the cases' coverage check, and the law's text supports leaving partners out. A later version should add partners if the directorate admits them (see OPEN-QUESTIONS). The Civil Code's rule for periods of years (arts. 339 and 340, in the chapter on juristic acts) is read as the convention for ages too, so a 29 February birthday counts on 28 February.

## Chile

Mercosur temporary residence is now route version `cl-mercosur-temporary` 1 in `packages/knowledge/data/routes`, verified on 2026-11-07 against the laws on Ley Chile (www.bcn.cl) and the migration service's pages on serviciomigraciones.cl. Chile grants it by reciprocity to nationals of the states that signed the 2002 agreement with it: Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay. Peru, Colombia, Ecuador and Venezuela are not on the list. It grants up to two years, can be extended, and is applied for online from abroad. Any native national of a listed state qualifies, a child included. A non-native citizenship of a listed state shows as possible, because Decreto 177 applies the agreement's own terms and the agreement counts a naturalized national only after five years. Its text is cited from Paraguay's approving law, since Chile's promulgating decree was not found on Ley Chile.

Dependents follow the migration law, art. 74, which applies to every temporary residence. A spouse, a partner in a genuine cohabiting relationship, and a child under 18 of the principal, the spouse or the partner are dependents. Older children qualify only with a disability, or when under 24 and studying at an institution Chile recognizes, and the case records neither, so they show as possible. Parents are not dependents of a temporary resident. They come in only through the separate family reunification subcategory (art. 19), which is not a route yet. A partner needs no minimum time together: the law asks only for a genuine relationship, which Decreto 177 (art. 73) proves with a sworn statement. Ages follow the Civil Code: of age at 18 (art. 26), and a period of years that starts on a day the end month lacks ends on that month's last day, a rule art. 48 also applies to ages, so a 29 February birthday counts on 28 February.

Not modeled, pending the owner's decision (see OPEN-QUESTIONS): art. 74 also requires the principal to prove a stable activity or income enough to support the dependents, which Decreto 177 (art. 74) measures against the Social Development Ministry's indicators. Those indicators are not in the law, and the version does not read them. The spouse and partner rules say so in their titles. The version counts the spouse, the partner and minor children as dependents without that proof, because a rule that can only answer "not known" never includes anyone and fails the cases' coverage check.

The law took effect when its regulation was published, on 2022-02-12, and the version starts when Decreto 177, which sets the subcategories, took effect on 2022-05-14. Ley Chile records no amendment to arts. 70 or 74 since then: the later amending laws (21.589, 21.590, 21.655, 21.730 and 21.816) change other articles.

## Latvia

Temporary residence for an investment in a company's share capital is now route version `lv-capital-investment` 1 in `packages/knowledge/data/routes`, verified on 2026-11-07 against the laws on likumi.lv. Latvia replaced its Immigration Law: the new one, adopted again on 2026-08-20 after the President returned it, took effect on 2026-09-15, which is the version's start. The [old law](https://likumi.lv/ta/id/68522-imigracijas-likums) lost force that day, as its page on likumi.lv shows. The investor grounds changed with it: buying real estate no longer gives a residence permit; the share-capital route stays, with the same amounts but a permit of up to two years instead of five; and a new route for EUR 150,000 placed for five years with a state-established alternative investment fund manager (art. 27(1) point 36) is not modeled: press reports quote the migration office saying the fund does not exist yet (**unverified**, pmlp.gov.lv did not answer the sandbox), and a bill sent to committee on 2026-09-10 would delete it ([LV portāls](https://lvportals.lv/skaidrojumi/394430-stajas-speka-jaunais-imigracijas-likums-2026), run by the official publisher).

The share-capital route needs EUR 50,000 invested in a company with at most 50 employees and turnover or balance sheet of at most EUR 10 million (at most 10 foreign investors per company), or EUR 100,000 in one with more than 50 employees (its Latvian subsidiaries counted) and turnover or balance sheet above EUR 10 million, plus EUR 10,000 paid once to the state budget. A company with more than 50 employees and turnover of EUR 10 million or less fits neither tier. The requirement reads the smaller tier: liquid assets of at least EUR 60,000, which the engine adds from the two amounts. The company's size and the yearly taxes it must pay for the permit to stay valid (EUR 40,000, or EUR 100,000 for the larger tier) are assessments, since the case records no company. Citizens of Russia and Belarus have no right to this permit; a dual citizen of either is read as excluded.

A family member gets a permit for the principal's term: the spouse, the minor children of the principal or the spouse (children under guardianship included), and persons under curatorship. Partners are not family members. The law gives this permit only to third-country nationals (art. 1(1) point 29: no EU, EEA or Swiss citizenship); version 2, added on 2026-11-07, corrects version 1 from its first day, since version 1 did not exclude a principal who also holds such a citizenship. Accepted gap: a person other than a child under the principal's curatorship, such as a parent, or a minor ward who is not recorded as a child, is not a dependent in this version, because a rule that can only answer "not known" fails the cases' coverage check. A minor is under 18 (Civil Law, art. 219), and a person who married before 18 is of age (art. 221), so a married minor is not a minor child. A minor the orphans' court declared of age from 16 (art. 220) is not recorded and reads as a minor. Adults under curatorship (art. 217: a court-limited capacity, or urgent curatorship without a limit) are not recorded, so adult children show as possible. Accepted reading: Latvian law has no rule for a 29 February birthday; the Administrative Procedure Law (art. 43(1)) ends a period whose last month lacks the date on that month's last day, and the version reads ages the same way (feb-28).

## Australia

The Skilled Independent visa (subclass 189), points-tested stream, is now route version `au-skilled-independent` 1 in `packages/knowledge/data/routes`, verified on 2026-11-07 against the Migration Regulations 1994 on the Federal Register of Legislation. It is a permanent visa for a skilled worker the Minister invites to apply, with no employer or state sponsor. The version starts on 2026-07-01, when the current visa application charges took effect; the clauses it reads are the same in the compilations in force from 2026-07-01 and 2026-10-02, and the amendments in force from 2026-11-01, 2026-11-03 and 2026-11-12, not yet compiled, touch other visas only.

The only check the engine makes is the age limit: the principal must not have turned 45 when invited, so the version reads a principal of 45 or more on the evaluation date as not eligible, and one under 45 must be invited before the 45th birthday. Accepted reading: the invitation date is not known in advance, so someone invited at 44 who turns 45 before applying still shows as not eligible on a later date. The invitation, the skilled occupation and its skills assessment, competent English and the points score are assessments: the case records none of them. The family unit applies together: the spouse, a de facto partner, and the children and stepchildren the regulations count (reg. 1.12(2)). A child with a recorded spouse or partner is not a family member, read through `person.hasSpouse` and the generic fact `person.hasPartner`, which reads the partner edges the case records. Accepted readings: an engaged child is not a family member either, and the case does not record an engagement; an unmarried child who may depend on the principal's spouse or partner, or who is 23 or more and cannot work, shows as possible; a citizenship of a Pacific-regional country is read as holding its passport, for the lower charges (the 13 countries are the country set `au-pacific-regional` 1, from reg. 1.03); and a 29 February birthday counts on 1 March, reading the Acts Interpretation Act's rule for ages (s. 37A) with its rule for a period that ends in a month without the start day (s. 2G(2)). The second instalment of the charge is listed for adult family members only: the principal must have competent English, a higher level than the functional English that avoids it.
