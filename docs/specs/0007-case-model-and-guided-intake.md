# Spec 0007: Case model, guided intake and rule activation

- Status: Ready for tickets
- Date: 2026-11-05
- Epic: consultancy core, slice 3 ([spec 0002](0002-rules-engine-and-knowledge-base.md#the-epic-consultancy-core)); the family area of epic [#117](https://github.com/fernandolisboa/arca/issues/117)
- Decisions: [ADR-0022](../adr/0022-single-approver-activation.md) (how a version becomes active), [ADR-0014](../adr/0014-knowledge-base-as-reviewed-data.md), [ADR-0008](../adr/0008-database-security-and-environments.md), [ADR-0015](../adr/0015-identity-enrollment-step-up-and-recovery.md), [ADR-0017](../adr/0017-data-lifecycle-and-backups.md)

## Goal

The family enters its case by answering guided questions, sees it played back in plain sentences, and gets each route's result for each person: who qualifies, who goes with whom, what is missing and which questions would change the answer. The owner activates or rejects the route versions those results use, with a passkey.

## Scope

**In:** case tables under forced row-level security; people, relationships, assets and who may see what; the family area (#118); guided questions per person; playback; evaluations and decisive questions; activating and rejecting route versions (ADR-0022); reporting a wrong rule; audit events, export, erasure and retention for all of it.

**Out, each a later step:**

- Free-text intake through the `Extractor` port, which needs an Anthropic API budget. The owner chose on 2026-11-05 to build guided questions first and free text after them. The guided questions stay as the way to correct what a model extracts.
- Uploading documents for another person (#119), which needs an ADR-0018 amendment.
- Aspirations, destination choice, comparison and shared analyses (slice 4, #120); checklists, plans and progress (slice 5, #121, #122); the guided tour (#123); FX rates (slice 7).
- The independent check agent (slice 2's shared tooling): the activation screen shows it as missing.

**Before real data enters:** the real-data gate ([runbook](../runbooks/real-data-gate.md)) has every item checked before the first screen that takes case data (ticket 4) is deployed to production; tickets 1 to 3 add tables and routes that hold nothing until then. Item 5, the first production run of the retention job, is the only one open on 2026-11-05. Tests, seeds and screenshots use synthetic cases only.

## Requirements

### R1. Case tables

A new schema `cases`, owned by `app_migrator`, with DML for `app_api`, the deletes and reads erasure and retention need for `app_jobs` (spec 0004 R9, R10), and every table under forced row-level security keyed on `app.account_id` (ADR-0008). Membership checks in policies go through one reviewed helper, `cases.is_member(case_id)`, so policies never recurse into `cases.member`. That helper, and every write that crosses keepers (joining, handing over, a co-owner's pending owner row, deleting an asset left without owners, the approvers' report counts), are `SECURITY DEFINER` functions owned by a dedicated role that cannot log in, with their own policies, a pinned `search_path` and their checks inside; ticket 1 lists them, since ADR-0008 forces row-level security on table owners too. Each table gets its entry in `packages/db/src/registry.ts` in the same pull request; where a table's owner is reached through a parent row or through either of two people (assets, relationships), ticket 1 extends the registry's owner type generically.

| Table | Holds | Visible to | Registry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cases.case_file` | ID and created time only | Its members | Unowned, no personal data; deleted when its last member leaves |
| `cases.member` | Case, account, the person row that is this account, joined time; the account is unique (one case per account in this slice) | Members of the same case | Owned by the account |
| `cases.person` | Case, display name (at most 80 characters), the person's own account if any, the keeping account (R2), whether the person is a member or a relative outside the case (such as a grandparent). A check keeps the person's own account equal to its keeper | Members of the same case | Owned by the keeping account |
| `cases.person_profile` | One row per person: the facts of the domain `personSchema` except `incomes`, `financiallyDependentOn` and the `assets` completeness marker, as JSON, plus whether the person's financial dependence was answered | The keeper, and accounts it shared the profile with | Owned by the keeper, through the person |
| `cases.person_finances` | One row per person: incomes, and whether the person's assets are recorded in full | The keeper, and accounts it shared finances with | Owned by the keeper, through the person |
| `cases.dependence` | Person, the person they depend on financially; both cascade on delete | Like the dependent person's profile | Owned through the dependent person |
| `cases.asset` | Kind, value, valuation date, liquidity, sale date | Whoever sees any of its owner rows | Owned through its confirmed owner rows; deleted, with any pending rows, when its last confirmed owner row goes |
| `cases.asset_owner` | Asset, person, share, and whether that person's keeper confirmed it; cascades on the person's deletion | Like that person's finances, and a pending row also to that person's keeper | Owned by the person's keeper |
| `cases.relationship` | One edge of the domain `relationshipSchema`, with person IDs; both ends cascade | Members of the same case | Owned through either person |
| `cases.share` | Person, grantee member (a foreign key to `cases.member` that cascades, so a member who leaves loses every share at once), scope (`profile` or `finances`) | The keeper and the grantee | Owned by the keeper |
| `cases.rule_report` | R8 | The reporter | Owned by the reporter |

- **References are rows.** Every reference from one person to another (dependence, asset ownership, relationships) is a row with a foreign key that cascades, never an ID inside JSON, so deleting a person leaves no dangling reference. The snapshot builder gives a person `financiallyDependentOn` only when the profile says dependence was answered (`[]` when answered with nobody), so "Não sei" stays unknown.
- **Validation.** Storage schemas in `packages/domain` derive from the snapshot's schemas with UUIDs in place of opaque IDs and without the cross-person fields above. The API validates every write with them, and validates a write to people, relationships, dependence or owner rows by building the case's people, relationships and owner rows as they would be after the write and running the snapshot's checks (no parent cycles, at most 4 parents, at most 4 couple edges per pair, no duplicate edge). Every write takes the case's transaction lock first, so two writes cannot pass these checks and the limits together. The checks that span rows a writer may not see (the asset count, and owner shares of an asset adding up to at most 1, pending rows included) run over every row of the case inside one of the `SECURITY DEFINER` functions, and their 409 says only that the limit is reached, never what a hidden row holds. No free text is stored except display names and report notes.
- **Limits.** The snapshot's limits apply per case: at most 30 members, 60 people, 200 relationships and 100 assets. A write that would exceed one answers 409.
- **Who writes.** Only the keeper writes a person's profile, finances, dependence and shares. Relationships are written by the keeper of either person in the edge. An asset row is written by the keeper of any confirmed owner; an owner row only by the keeper of that person. A keeper may name a co-owner they do not keep, which adds a pending owner row that the co-owner's keeper sees and confirms or declines ("Bruno é dono de metade deste imóvel?"); the engine counts only confirmed rows, so nobody puts an asset into someone else's finances.
- As built (#228): the `cases` schema with every table above but `cases.rule_report`, which comes with ticket 10 (as built in #236, under R8). A member's own person is the person row whose account is theirs (unique), so `cases.member` holds no person column. The keeper is a foreign key to `cases.member` that cascades, so a member who leaves takes the people they keep, and every row that names two people or a person and an asset carries the case ID in composite foreign keys, so nothing links across cases. JSON columns hold the storage schemas' shapes (`facts`, `incomes`, a relationship's `details`) with a type and size check; amounts are `bigint`, shares `numeric(7,6)` between 0 and 1. The role `cases_definer` (bootstrap creates it without login; `app_migrator` may only `SET ROLE` to it, to hand it functions) owns `cases.is_member`, and the triggers `drop_unowned_asset` (after an owner row goes) and `drop_empty_case` (after a member goes), each taking a transaction lock on its asset or case so two concurrent deletes of the last rows still remove it (under READ COMMITTED; two multi-row deletes that meet the same assets in opposite orders can deadlock, and the definer functions and the erasure job retry on `40P01`); `app_api` may execute only `is_member`. A member's own person is deleted only by leaving, never directly. `app_api` writes, under the policies, people it keeps without an account, their profiles, finances, dependence and shares, relationships of a person it keeps, an asset it confirmed ownership of, and the confirmation of its own pending owner rows; it cannot insert cases, members, assets or owner rows, nor change keepers or accounts. Those, with the cross-row checks, are the definer functions the case API adds (as built in #229, under R3): create a case, hand over, add an asset with its owner row, name a co-owner or change a share in an asset; joining comes with ticket 3. The registry's owners gain any of several parents (relationships) and the rows that reference an entry (assets, through any of their owner rows, pending ones included, as row-level security shows them; the trigger removes an asset with its last confirmed row). The guards confine a definer role to its own schema. The export carries the case rows' scalar columns; the JSON facts, amounts and shares join it with ticket 11 (as built in #237, under R9). The API's every-table fixture and `tooling/scripts/test/postgres/case-access.test.ts` hold the matrix below.
- Access-matrix tests cover every table: a member of another case sees nothing; a member sees names and relationships but no profile or finances until shared; revoking a share hides the row at once; only the keeper writes; a member who leaves loses every share; `app_auth` has no access; `app_jobs` reaches only what erasure and retention delete.

### R2. Members, invites and the family area

- **Keeper.** Each person has one keeping account. An adult with an account keeps their own person. A person without an account (a child, or a relative who never signs in) is kept by the account that added them.
- **Creating a case.** A confirmed account (spec 0003 R2) creates its case and its own person row in one call; it becomes the first member.
- **Joining.** The invite flow of spec 0003 is the only way in. After the inviter confirms an invitee, the screen offers to add them to the case: `POST /v1/case/members` with `{accountId, displayName}` or `{accountId, personId}`, allowed only when a confirmed `account.link` joins the caller and that account and the account is in no case yet, and idempotent, so a retry after a lost answer is safe. With `personId`, the invitee takes over a person the inviter keeps and that has no account (such as a parent the inviter already described); that needs a `sensitive_settings` step-up with the person as a target, and the inviter's shares of that person are dropped, so the new keeper decides who sees their data.
- **Handing over.** A keeper hands a person without an account to another member the same way (step-up, shares dropped), for example before leaving.
- **Leaving.** A member leaves the case from the family area after a `sensitive_settings` step-up. The adult takes their data: every person they keep is deleted from the case, with their facts, their owner rows, dependence and relationships through the cascades, and assets with no owner left. A last member leaving deletes the case.
- **The family area** (#118) lists the case's people with their relationships in plain words, whether each has an account and their link state (invited, waiting for confirmation, member), and, for the people the viewer keeps, who sees their profile and finances. From a person's row the viewer invites that relative (spec 0003 invites), opens the person's guided questions, hands the person over, or changes sharing.
- As built (#230): `POST /v1/case/members` takes `{accountId, displayName}` for a new person or `{accountId, personId}` to take one over, since an account has no name of its own to start the person with. The API reads the `account.link` under the caller's identity, then calls the definer function `cases.join_case`, which takes the case's lock, refuses an account in another case and a person the caller does not keep or that has an account, and answers an account already in the caller's case unchanged. The link is read by the API, not by the function, so `cases_definer` stays confined to its schema (guard 6); `app_api` sets the identity anyway, so a check inside the function would not stop a compromised API. The size limits are the API's, like the others every member can count. A join writes the audit event `member_joined`, with the case and the added account, to both accounts' trails, since the added account did not ask for it. `GET /v1/invites` names a confirmed invite's account, and the invites screen offers to add it right there once the inviter has a case: as someone new with a name, or as a person the inviter described who has no account yet (the step-up then), with the dropped shares said up front.
- As built (#118): `/family` in the main navigation. Without a case it starts one with the viewer's name. Each person shows whether they are in the planning (`caseMember`) or a relative outside it, whether they have an account (as a case member), their relationships in plain words, and, for people the viewer keeps, who sees their profile and finances; for the others, what their keeper shares with the viewer (`sharedWithMe` in the case view, read from the viewer's own share rows). From a kept person's row the viewer changes sharing, renames or sets the planning flag, hands a person without an account over (step-up), removes them, or opens the invites; a person just added opens the sharing question at once, every member unchecked. The area also adds a person, notes or removes a relationship, lists the viewer's invites with their state, and leaves the case after a step-up. The grantee's notice is in the area itself: what this device last saw (member account IDs and `person:scope` pairs, never names, in local storage) is compared with the case, so a member who joined, and each share started or stopped, shows once until dismissed; a device that never looked at this case starts from what it sees, with nothing sent over the network. So the notice is a convenience, not a record: a new device, cleared storage, or a share granted and taken back between two visits shows nothing; the audit events are the record. An invite is not tied to a person yet, so a row's invite action opens the invites screen and the invite states are listed by email, not on each person's row. The area links to the guided questions (#231).
- **Sharing.** The keeper shares a person's profile and, separately, finances, with chosen members. Nothing is shared until the keeper chooses: the first time a keeper saves a person, the screen asks who may see it, with all members listed and none preselected. A member who joins later is asked about by each keeper in the family area. Granting and revoking write audit events and show the grantee a notice in the app.

### R3. API

- Every route is declared in `packages/api-contract` with Zod schemas built from the storage schemas, runs as `app_api` with `withIdentity`, and refuses an unconfirmed account (spec 0003 R2).
- Routes, as a minimum: read the case (people, relationships, and what the viewer may see and edit); create the case; add a member (R2); add, rename, hand over and remove a person; write a profile, finances, dependence, an asset, an owner row and a relationship; replace a person's shares; leave the case; list the next questions (R4); read the playback (R5); list evaluations (R6); report a wrong rule (R8). Writes return the updated view, so the client never merges state itself.
- Person and asset IDs are UUIDs in the API and the database. The snapshot builder replaces them with opaque IDs (`p1`, `a1`) for the engine, and maps results back.
- Display names never appear in URLs, logs, error reports or audit details (CLAUDE.md privacy rules); request logs carry route IDs and status only, as today.
- Rate limits (spec 0003 R10): evaluations and playback 60 per hour per session; writes 300 per hour per account.
- As built (#229): the routes of `packages/api-contract/src/routes/case.ts` (the contract now depends on `packages/domain` for the storage schemas), served by `apps/api/src/case/`.
  - **Lock.** Every write takes a transaction advisory lock on the case, under the key the empty-case cleanup locks, in place of `FOR UPDATE`, so `app_api` needs no `UPDATE` on `cases.case_file`.
  - **Definer functions.** `cases_definer` owns four more functions, each taking the same lock: `create_case`, `hand_over` (which drops the person's shares), `add_asset` (the asset with its first confirmed owner row, at most 100 per case) and `set_owner` (a co-owner's pending row, or the keeper's own share; at most 12 owner rows per asset, shares at most 1, pending rows included). A limit raises SQLSTATE `CL001`, which the API answers as `409 case_limit`.
  - **Leaving.** A member leaves by deleting their own `cases.member` row under the policy `member_delete_self`. The cascades and the triggers do the rest, so leaving needs no definer function.
  - **Shares.** Changing shares is an insert or delete by the keeper under the policies. The foreign key to `cases.member` keeps grantees in the case.
  - **Error codes.** `404 not_found` for anything the viewer may not write, `409 case_exists`, `case_limit` and `case_invalid`.
  - **Relationships.** A relationship is removed and added again rather than edited.
  - **Not here yet.** The grantee's in-app notice of a share came with the family area (ticket 4, #118, under R2); `member_joined` came with ticket 3 (#230, under R2).
  - **Snapshot builder.** It is `buildCaseSnapshot` in `packages/domain/src/case/builder.ts`, with `validCaseStructure` for the checks across rows.

### R4. Guided questions

- The question flow is data in `packages/domain`: an ordered list of steps (basics, studies and languages, work, finances, immigration history, family), each with questions that set one fact path of the storage schemas and an input kind. A new fact in the schema adds a question there without touching screens.
- The API answers the next questions for a person, in flow order, with what is already answered; the client renders each by its step and question IDs, with text through i18n. Each step saves on its own, so a person can stop and come back.
- Unknown is always an answer: "Não sei" leaves the fact absent, which the engine treats as unknown (spec 0002 R3), and partial dates (year, or year and month) are accepted where the schema accepts them.
- Completeness questions set the markers: "Esses são todos os filhos de Ana?" sets `children` only when answered yes; the assets question sets the finances marker.
- Inputs are touch-first and responsive (spec 0006); countries come from the pinned country dataset with pt-BR names. Every string goes through i18n in natural pt-BR (CLAUDE.md language rule), written plain and short.
- As built (#231):
  - Domain: `questionFlow` in `packages/domain/src/case/questions.ts` lists 17 questions over the six steps, each with a target (a profile fact, a completeness marker, dependence, incomes, the assets marker, the person's assets, the person's relationships' details) and an input kind (partial or full date, with "never" where the schema takes `none`; country, with former states for citizenships; ISO 639-1 language; choice from a named value set; yes or no; money; share; list of items; people; complete; relationship details). A test checks the flow against the storage schemas' keys, list items included. `applyAnswers` (pure) turns the answers into the stored shapes and checks them against the storage schemas and the pinned codes; `heldAssetChanges` works out the asset writes. Amounts travel as decimal strings in the currency's units and become minor units in the domain.
  - API: `GET /case/questions` answers every person the viewer keeps at once, so no person ID travels in a URL, with each question's answer and, for a completeness question, the people it is about; the countries come from the latest pinned `countries` and `former-countries` files, which the knowledge build now emits beside the catalog. `POST /case/questions/answers` saves one step in one transaction: the profile, finances, the assets the person holds a confirmed share of, and the relationship details that changed. An asset the answer does not name stays as it is, and one the person no longer holds is sent as `{ id, dropped: true }`, which removes only the person's owner row, so a form opened before another tab added an asset never drops it. Case bodies are capped at 256 KiB.
  - Screen: `/family/questions`, linked from the family area: a person picker when the viewer keeps several, the steps with their progress, one form per step, opened at the first step with a question still open. The client only moves the decimal comma (a point is a thousands separator only between groups of three) and turns a percentage into a share by moving the point; a half-typed date is refused by the browser before the step is sent.
  - Limits: assets have no "Não sei" (a person's assets are rows, not a fact), and co-owners are still named from the asset routes, not here. A completeness question answered "Pode haver mais" stores nothing, since only yes sets a marker, so it reads back as unanswered. A step saved from a stale form writes back the fields it shows, so a co-owner's edit to a shared asset or relationship made after the form opened is overwritten.

### R5. Playback

- A pure function in `packages/domain`, `describeCase(snapshot, visibility, at)`, returns the case as sentence descriptors: a message key and typed parameters (person IDs, dates, countries, amounts), never text. `visibility` says, per person, whether the viewer sees the profile and the finances, so "not shared" reads apart from "not answered". Examples: two people married since a date; a child of two parents with shared custody; a person who holds two citizenships; an asset and its owners' shares; a refusal in a country and year.
- The API returns the descriptors with each one's question ID. The client renders them through i18n with the viewer's names for the IDs and links each sentence to its question, so correcting a sentence is one tap.
- Table-driven tests cover every descriptor kind, partial dates and both "not shared" and "not answered".
- As built (#232):
  - Domain: `describeCase` in `packages/domain/src/case/playback.ts` returns sentences checked by `sentenceSchema`: each a `kind`, the question that sets it, `about` (the people whose question it opens) and typed values (dates as stored, partial ones included; amounts as decimal strings in the currency's units; ages as a range of whole years at `at`, computed in the domain). Per person, in flow order: "not shared" for a profile or finances the viewer does not see, then each fact, an empty list as "none", and "not answered" for a fact, dependence or incomes left out; then completeness markers; then the relationships and the assets with their owners' shares. `renamePeople` swaps the snapshot's opaque IDs for the case's.
  - API: `GET /case/playback` builds the viewer's snapshot and their visibility (keeping a person, or a share of that scope), so a shared profile nobody filled in reads as not answered and one never shared as not shared. It counts 60 per hour per session (`case_playback_session`). An asset with no confirmed owner row the viewer sees is left out, as in evaluations, though the family area lists it.
  - Screen: the guided questions screen has "Ver o resumo do caso": each person's sentences, then "Vínculos" and "Bens", worded through i18n with the viewer's names, dates and money formatted by `Intl`. A sentence about someone the viewer keeps is a button that opens that person's step, so the person and question never travel in a URL.

### R6. Evaluations and decisive questions

- `GET /v1/case/evaluations` builds a snapshot from the rows the viewer may see. Row-level security does the filtering, so a hidden fact is an unknown fact; an asset is in the snapshot with the confirmed owner rows the viewer sees, and not at all without one, so a co-owner's hidden share stays hidden, and the builder leaves out the `assets` completeness marker of a person whose finances the viewer does not see, so hidden assets read as unknown, never as none. It computes decision states (R7) and runs `evaluateRoute` for every route in the catalog with each member of the case as principal.
- Context: `recordedOn`, `rulesAsOf` and `factsAsOf` are today's date in São Paulo, `today` the same; the `FxTable` is empty in this slice, so a comparison across currencies reports its missing rate apart (spec 0002 R5) until slice 7 adds rates.
- The response gives, per route and principal: the status, the `stale` flag and any `newerProposedVersion`, the unit, each requirement's result with its sources and verification dates, costs and durations as the engine reports them, and the decisive missing facts.
- **Only the questions that change the answer.** A pure function in `packages/domain` maps each decisive missing fact to the question that sets it (R4) and orders the questions by how many route and principal pairs they could decide, then by flow order. A fact about a person the viewer does not keep becomes "ask Ana to answer this" instead of a question.
- Evaluations are computed on request and not stored in this slice; the response carries each version's identity and the engine commit, so a result can be reproduced (ADR-0014).
- The results screen lists routes by country, each with a status per person using the glossary's labels (Elegível, Não elegível, Faltam informações, Depende de critério subjetivo, Versão aguardando ativação, Indisponível), a stale-source note where it applies, who goes with whom, and the questions above. Every rule shown keeps its source and date (CLAUDE.md rule 1).
- As built (#235): `GET /v1/case/evaluations` counts 60 per hour per session (`case_evaluations_session`), reads the decision states before the case's transaction, and evaluates every route `@arca/knowledge` bundles (`routeIds`) with each member as principal. `routeResult` and `decisiveQuestions` live in `packages/domain/src/case/results.ts`: each decisive missing fact, each possible dependent's and each completeness marker the unit lacks maps to the question that sets it (`questionForFact`, typed over every fact key), counted once per route and principal pair; a relationship's question, or a relationship detail's, goes to an end the viewer keeps when there is one, and a lineage fact about a child's birth asks the next person in the line. A route a requirement rules out, or a closed one, asks nothing, since no answer changes its status. The API names each result field by field, so the missing facts and the snapshot's opaque IDs never leave it (costs too, field by field), maps the opaque IDs back to the case's UUIDs, and parses the answer with the contract's schema. Only an active version's title, country and closure sources are in the answer (rule 5); a route with none shows its ID under "Outras rotas". The exchange rates the empty FX table lacks and the reference dates the case does not set are listed apart, as notes, since no question answers them. The engine commit is `VERCEL_GIT_COMMIT_SHA`, or `unknown` off Vercel. The screen is `/family/results`, linked from the family area: the questions first, the one about a person the viewer keeps linking to the guided questions and the others asking whoever keeps that person, then routes by country name and title, each member's status as a chip, the stale and newer-version notes, who goes with whom, and, folded under each status, the requirements, assessments, costs that apply or may apply, and durations, each with a source line linking the source's host and its verification date; a closed route says "Rota encerrada" with its closure's sources.
  - Limits: cost totals are not in the answer yet. A person whose profile the viewer does not see always reads as unknown, so their questions read "ask whoever keeps them" even when that keeper already answered. Each principal's result repeats its version's requirements and sources, which is fine at a few routes.

### R7. Activation and rejection (ADR-0022)

- **Approvers** are listed per environment in a reviewed file in `apps/api` by account ID. Production lists the owner's account. Previews and development list the synthetic seed account.
- **Decision.** An approver activates or rejects one version identity (route ID, number, content hash as lowercase hex) with a step-up of the new purpose `knowledge_decision` (spec 0003 R5; the purpose joins the contract's enum and the database's purpose check): action `activateVersion` or `rejectVersion`, targets the route ID and version number, field `contentHash`. The route answers 403 to an account not on the list, takes the content hash from the catalog the API bundles, refuses an unknown version or one already rejected, and consumes the step-up; the step-up consumer returns the consumed assertion and reads the passkey's public key in the same `app_auth` transaction, for the copy below.
- **Storage.** `knowledge.decision` is self-contained, so a decision can be verified again after its passkey is revoked or its step-up row is gone: ID; kind; route ID; version number; content hash; approver account ID, as a plain value with no foreign key into `auth`; credential ID and its COSE public key, copied when the step-up is consumed; the client's action nonce; the step-up's server nonce, authenticator data, client data JSON and signature; the step-up ID, unique; and decided time. Insert-only: `app_auth` inserts it in the transaction that consumes the step-up, `app_api` may select, nobody may update or delete, as for the audit log. Registry: a retained entry on the approver account ID, never erased, as the record of which rules are in use (ticket 7 fits it to the registry's types); it holds no other personal data. Each decision also writes an audit event.
- **Verification.** Before a decision counts, the API checks that its approver is on the list and that its stored assertion verifies: it rebuilds the subject hash from the decision's own kind, route ID, version number, content hash and action nonce, the challenge from that hash and the server nonce, and checks it with spec 0003 R5's re-verification function against the stored public key. A verified result is cached by decision ID, since decisions never change. An activation that fails is ignored and logged. A rejection counts even if it fails, so a fault can only take a rule out of use, never put one in.
- **States.** A pure, table-tested function in `packages/domain`, in its own module, computes each identity's state: rejected if any rejection counts, else active if an activation counts, else proposed. The API passes the result to the engine as `states` (spec 0002 R5).
- **The activation screen**, shown to approvers only, lists proposed versions, newest first. For one version the API returns a view built by a pure function in `packages/domain` from the version's content, read through a display-only catalog entry point that never feeds an evaluation (the boundary rule and spec 0002 R5 name it), with no model involved: the rule's requirements as a tree with fact keys, its dependents, costs and durations, its pinned country sets with their members, the difference from the version it replaces, each source with its full URL as plain text, its title, kind and verification date, with hosts new to the route flagged, its rule cases with their expected results, and the content hash. The client only renders it, with fact names in pt-BR. The independent check's panel says the check does not exist yet.
- Rejecting an active version shows the routes that would fall back to an older version or to Versão aguardando ativação, and asks for confirmation.
- As built (#233): `POST /v1/knowledge/versions/activate` and `/reject` take `{routeId, number}` and answer `{decisionId}`, or 409 `already_rejected`; the step-up action is the route's operation ID (`activateVersion`, `rejectVersion`), so a step-up for one kind never spends for the other. The approver list is `apps/api/src/knowledge/approvers.ts`; production lists the owner's account (added on 2026-11-06). The step-up's `spend` updates the row, reads the passkey's public key and inserts the decision in one `app_auth` transaction, so a step-up is spent only with its decision; the audit event is committed on `app_audit` just before the insert, so a decision never lacks its event, though an event may name a decision whose insert then failed. A row carries its own public key, so whoever may insert one could sign it; only `app_auth` may, and its policy admits only a row that copies a spent `knowledge_decision` step-up of the approver, so forging a decision needs the credential role, as ADR-0022 states. `app_api` reads every decision, since every evaluation needs the states. `knowledge.decision` has no foreign key; the registry's retained period may now be `forever` (spec 0004 R7). The states function is `decisionStates` in `packages/domain/src/rules/decisions.ts`: a rejection counts whether or not it verifies, an activation only when its approver is listed and its assertion verifies. The API checks each decision once per process and keeps the checked decision, built from the row it verified, by ID; an ignored activation is logged once. Re-verification checks the assertion against this deployment's origin, so a decision made on one branch's previews does not count on another branch's, which share the development database; production has one origin, and moving it to another host would leave every activation uncounted until each version is activated again.
- As built (#234): `GET /v1/knowledge/versions` lists every bundled version with its state, the newest first, and `GET /v1/knowledge/versions/view?routeId=…&number=…` returns the view; both answer 403 `not_approver` to anyone else, and route IDs are public catalog data, never personal data. `versionView` lives in `packages/domain/src/rules/version-view.ts`. The view lists each requirement's, dependent rule's and cost's expression with every fact node resolved to its catalog key and target and every money amount in the currency's units (the domain's ISO 4217 exponents, never the client's); the version's settings (leap-day birthday rule and its sources, reference dates, closure sources, pinned files, tags); and what changed from the version it replaces (the one it corrects, else the one before): items added, removed or changed per section by ID, and a `settings` section naming every other field that differs, so the screen calls two versions equal only when everything but their numbers and `corrects` is. Each source shows the host the build's official-source matching reads (`@arca/scripts/host`, emitted into the catalog module), or a warning when the URL has no plain host, which also counts as new to the route. Rule cases show their expected results without their snapshots; the cases summaries reach the API only through `@arca/knowledge/display`, whose lint rule also bans relative imports and re-exports. What rejecting would do is computed for today in São Paulo: whether the route selects this version now, and what it would select instead; a version that only takes effect later shows no fallback. The screen is `/knowledge`, linked from the settings only when the session says the account is an approver (`approver` in `GET /v1/session`); it shows proposed versions first and the others below, where an active version can be rejected. It names every operator, fact, target, collection and relationship step in pt-BR, and a web test holds the labels to the domain's lists (`expressionOperators`, `factKeys`, `factTargets`, `collectionNames`, `relationshipSteps`). Source URLs are plain text, never links.

### R8. Reporting a wrong rule

- From a requirement shown in a result, a member marks it as possibly wrong, with a reason from a fixed list (outdated, wrong amount, wrong condition, source does not say this, other) and an optional note of at most 500 characters.
- The server takes the route ID, version number and requirement key by recomputing the reporter's own evaluation and finding the requirement in it; a report about a requirement not in it answers 404. At most 20 reports per account per day.
- `cases.rule_report` holds the report and its state (`open`, `closed`); the note is case data under row-level security. Approvers see open reports through one reviewed `SECURITY DEFINER` function, which the API calls only for an approver and which returns route ID, version number, requirement key, reason and count, never a note, a case or a reporter. An approver closes the reports of a requirement once it is researched; activating a correction of that version closes them too.
- Nothing sends a report to an agent or a model; the owner asks an agent to research it.
- As built (#236): `POST /v1/case/rule-reports` takes `{routeId, versionNumber, requirementId, reason, note?}` and answers `{}`, or 404 when the caller's own evaluation, recomputed under its identity, shows no such requirement of that active version; it counts 20 per account per 24 hours (`rule_report_account`) and writes the audit event `rule_reported` with the report, route and version, never the note. Only the reporter reads or inserts its rows (insert also needs case membership), so other members never see a note; `app_jobs` deletes them with the account and after 365 days (docs/RETENTION.md), and the export carries them. Approvers read `GET /v1/knowledge/reports` and close with `POST /v1/knowledge/reports/close` (`{routeId, versionNumber, requirementId}`, answering `{closed}`; 403 `not_approver` to anyone else), through the definer functions `cases.open_rule_reports()` and `cases.close_rule_reports(route, version, requirement)`, which return or touch only the grouped counts. Storing an activation of a version that `corrects` another closes every open report of the corrected version. The result screen offers the report under each requirement of an active version; the activation screen lists open reports by requirement, with a count per reason.

### R9. Audit, export, erasure and retention

- Audit events, with IDs and kinds only, never names or facts: case created, member joined, member left, person added, person removed, person handed over, share granted, share revoked, rule reported, version activated, version rejected.
- Data export (spec 0004 R8) includes every case row the account keeps and the case's people and relationships it can see.
- Erasing an account (spec 0004 R9) does what leaving does (R2) first. The deletion screen warns that people the account keeps, such as children, are deleted with it unless they are handed over first.
- Retention: case rows live as long as their case and their keeper's account; `cases.rule_report` rows are deleted 365 days after creation; decisions are kept. [RETENTION.md](../RETENTION.md) gains these lines.
- As built (#237): the export (schema version 2) carries the case rows' JSON values as their JSON text (profile facts, incomes, relationship details) with asset amounts and owner shares as exact decimal text, such as `"1000"` and `"1.000000"`, and a `case` section: every person and relationship the account sees, its own and other keepers', named by display name, never by ID; null outside a case. Erasure deletes the member row first, the registry's first case entry, so its cascade takes the people the account keeps, as leaving does, and the other members keep a case whose snapshot still builds; the trail records `account_erased`, not `member_left`, since a retried erasure step could not record the leave again. The deletion screen's confirmation names the people the account keeps, other than its own person, and links to the family area to hand them over first.

## Threats

| Threat | Control |
| --- | --- |
| A relative reads another adult's finances | Profile and finances in their own tables under row-level security, shared only by the keeper; evaluations see only what the viewer sees, and hidden assets read as unknown, so a result never reveals a hidden fact; access-matrix tests |
| Another family's data leaks across cases | Every policy keys on case membership through one helper; access-matrix case with two cases |
| Someone puts data into another adult's record | Only the keeper writes a person's rows; owner rows only by that person's keeper; access-matrix tests |
| A keeper deletes a jointly owned asset | Accepted: the keeper of any confirmed owner may delete it, as R1 says; the other owners' keepers see it gone and can add it again; a family app does not need a second approval here |
| A co-owner learns about owner rows they may not see | Accepted: the shares check counts rows the co-owner may not see, so changing their own share until it is refused reveals the others' total, and naming a person who already owns the asset is refused; neither shows a hidden amount, and in a family app that is not worth a second rule |
| A forged or replayed decision activates a wrong rule | Step-up bound to the content hash; approver list in the repository; a self-contained decision verified again before it counts; insert-only table. Residual in ADR-0022 |
| A lost passkey brings back a rejected rule | Decisions carry their own public key, and a rejection counts even when it cannot be verified |
| Names leak into logs or analytics | Names only in `cases.person`; logs carry route IDs; audit details carry IDs and kinds |
| A report note or a display name steers an agent | Neither reaches an agent or a model in this slice |
